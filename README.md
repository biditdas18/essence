---

**Current status (October 2026).** This project's original framing --- "Essence recovers long-tail content better than collaborative filtering" --- did not survive evaluation, and neither did its first correction. Two findings stand. First, peer-isolated retrieval's standing is governed by **the density of cross-user co-occurrence in the evaluated cohort, not by the content domain**: it ranks 2nd and 3rd of ten where that density is low (Last.fm-1K, Amazon Books) and 8th of ten where it is high (MovieLens-25M), and the mechanism is demonstrated *within a single dataset* --- lifting Last.fm-1K's user cap from 99 to 374 users raises the share of items heard by two or more users from 10.3% to 28.4% and reverses the verdict against item-based collaborative filtering ($d = +0.281 \to -0.160$, both significant). An earlier version of this README called the pattern "domain-dependence"; that was measuring the cohort and naming the domain. Second, and centrally, **$K$-means interest clustering contributes nothing measurable** over simple recency-weighted retrieval: none of 18 silhouette-stratified comparisons at the adopted $K$ favour clustering, and under study-wide correction across 136 tests no comparison anywhere favours it. This README documents the current picture --- the wins, the losses, the null, and the corrections --- not the original favourable framing.

## What Essence Is

Essence is a personal recommendation framework that operates exclusively within a single user's own engagement history, without consulting cross-user interaction data ("peer isolation"). A user's consumed items are embedded with a pre-trained sentence encoder (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`), clustered into $K$ semantic centroids via $K$-means, and recommendations are generated by retrieving unseen items similar (cosine similarity) to whichever centroid is closest to the user's most recent activity.

**This repository is structured as an evaluation study, not a method-introduction project**: the central question is whether $K$-means interest clustering, specifically, provides measurable benefit over a simpler recency-based alternative, tested under conditions designed to give clustering its best chance to matter. The central empirical question is not "does Essence work" but **"under what domain conditions does peer-isolated, content-only personalization help versus hurt, and is that association predictive?"** (it is not yet shown to be — see [Key Findings](#key-findings)) — see [Headline Results](#headline-results-all-three-datasets) below.

## $K$-Selection Methodology

$K$ is selected per dataset on a validation split carved from training data (the last 20% of each user's chronological training history; the held-out test set is never touched during selection), swept over $\{2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10, 15, 20, 30, 50\}$.

The criterion is **anchoring-free by construction**: choose the $K$ maximising validation Recall@10 subject to (a) mean items per cluster $\geq 3$ and (b) every user clusterable. Constraint (a) rules out degenerate singleton clusters; (b) rules out a configuration whose apparent quality is partly produced by users silently falling back to a non-clustered path.

| Dataset | Earlier $K$ | Adopted $K$ | Why it changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last.fm-1K | 10 | 10 | unchanged |
| Amazon Books | 10 | **8** | $K{=}10$ gives 2.58 items per cluster, below the floor of 3 |
| MovieLens-25M | 15 | **10** | at $K{=}15$ only ~90% of users could cluster |

This replaces an earlier stopping rule that was evaluated *forward from an already-established $K{=}8$ checkpoint* — correctly identified by a reviewer as anchoring. Two further defects in the published configuration surfaced when the rule was replaced: Amazon Books at $K{=}10$ sat below the repository's own declared occupancy floor, and on MovieLens-25M a tenth of validation users could not cluster at all and fell back to a different method, by a margin larger than the winning one. Both are now excluded by construction rather than disclosed.

The adopted values are invariant to the sentence encoder (`all-mpnet-base-v2` selects the same $K$ as `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` on every dataset). They are **not** a property of the domain: on Amazon Books the same criterion selects $K{=}8$ for users with ~29 interactions and $K{=}60$ for users with 330. Any single reported $K$ is contingent on the user filter. Full sweep data: `results/validation_k_sweep_{lastfm,amazon,movielens}.csv` and `results/k_selection_final.csv`.

| **Amazon Books** | E-commerce | 2,000 (subsampled) | 61,727 | `data/amazon_raw/` (not committed) → `data/amazon_processed/` (row-level `train.csv`/`test.csv` **not committed**, gitignored — regenerate via the script in [Reproducing the Results](#reproducing-the-results); catalog-only `item_meta.csv` remains committed) |
| **MovieLens-25M** | Film | 2,000 (subsampled) | 7,654 (with fetched TMDb overview text) | `data/movielens_raw/` (not committed) → `data/movielens_processed/` (row-level `train.csv`/`test.csv`/`sampled_ratings.csv` **not committed**, gitignored — regenerate via the scripts in [Reproducing the Results](#reproducing-the-results); catalog-only `item_candidates.csv`/`item_text.csv`/TMDb files remain committed) |

All three use a **chronological 80/20 split per user** (first 80% of a user's interactions by timestamp = train, last 20% = test), computed independently per user with no shared global time cutoff — required for consistency with Essence's recency-based active-cluster-selection mechanism, which defines "recent" in terms of training-set order. **This does not prevent cross-user temporal leakage** for population-based baselines (CF, Popularity): an item's co-occurrence signal can include other users' interactions that occurred, in wall-clock time, after a given user's personal train/test cutoff. This is a disclosed limitation of the evaluation protocol, not eliminated by the per-user split.

A positive interaction is any recorded play (Last.fm-1K), rating or review (Amazon Books), or rating (MovieLens-25M) — no minimum count or rating-value threshold beyond the per-user interaction-count filters (50–500 Last.fm-1K, 20–200 Amazon/MovieLens). This evaluates engagement/consumption prediction, not preference prediction specifically. A positive-rating threshold (rating ≥ 4) **was** tested as a robustness check (see [Known Limitations](#known-limitations) and the overnight robustness-check batch in [Reproducing the Results](#reproducing-the-results)) — it did not reverse the cohort-density or clustering-null findings, though sample sizes drop 20–28% once users below the stricter floor are excluded.

Long-tail items are defined per dataset as training-set singletons (interaction count exactly 1): 16,761/18,679 (89.7%) on Last.fm-1K, 42,878/50,924 (84.2%) on Amazon Books, 2,337/6,745 (34.6%) on MovieLens-25M. LT-Recall@10 eligible users (at least one long-tail test item): 92 of 99 on Last.fm-1K, 1,292 of 2,000 on Amazon Books, 490 of 2,000 on MovieLens-25M.

## Systems Compared

| System | Query construction |
|---|---|
| Random | Uniform random over unseen items |
| Popularity | Most globally-interacted-with unseen items (non-personalized) |
| CF (ItemKNN) | Item-based cosine-weighted aggregate over the user-item co-occurrence matrix; no neighbor-count cutoff or shrinkage (full-neighborhood, $k=\infty$) |
| Content (Avg Emb) | Cosine similarity to the mean of all history embeddings |
| **Essence (adopted $K$)** | Cosine similarity to the active $K$-means centroid ($K{=}10$ Last.fm-1K, $K{=}8$ Amazon Books, $K{=}10$ MovieLens-25M) |
| Last-Item | Cosine similarity to the single most recent item |
| Avg-Last-10 | Cosine similarity to the uniform mean of the last 10 items |
| Recency-Weighted | Cosine similarity to an exponentially-decayed mean (decay $=0.9$) of the last 10 items |
| MIND | Dynamic-routing multi-interest capsules ($K{=}4$), hand-implemented — not available in standard recommendation libraries |
| ComiRec (SA) | Self-attentive multi-interest capsules ($K{=}4$), hand-implemented |

Last-Item, Avg-Last-10, and Recency-Weighted use the *identical* item embeddings as Content and Essence — the only difference across those five systems is how the query vector is built from recent history. This isolates whether Essence's clustering step adds anything beyond simple recency weighting (see [Key Findings](#key-findings)).

MIND and ComiRec are verified against their architectural specifications rather than against the original papers' published benchmark numbers, since neither paper's evaluation protocol (sampled-negative, different datasets) matches this repo's full-catalog, no-negative-sampling, chronological-split protocol; both are hand-implemented since neither is available in standard recommendation libraries (RecBole was checked directly and confirmed not to ship either). Both are trained with Adam (lr $=10^{-3}$), batch size 64, up to 50 epochs with early stopping (patience 5) on held-out-last-interaction validation loss, and a sampled-softmax loss (100 uniform-random negatives per positive). See [MIND/ComiRec training and verification](#4-mindcomirec-training-and-verification) below for how correctness is checked.

## Headline Results, All Three Datasets

Full per-dataset tables are in the camera-ready paper (`paper/essence_soict_cameraready.pdf`). Summary at each dataset's adopted $K$, with every baseline reported at **the better of its published and its validation-tuned configuration** — a convention that deliberately works against Essence:

| Dataset | Users | Adopted $K$ | Essence Recall@10 | Rank of 10 | Significant / tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last.fm-1K | 99 | 10 | 0.028075 | **2nd** | 6 / 18 |
| Amazon Books | 2,000 | 8 | 0.024619 | **3rd** | 10 / 18 |
| MovieLens-25M | 2,000 | 10 | 0.005511 | **8th** | 7 / 18 |

The ordering holds under Recall@5, Recall@20 and NDCG@10 on the two datasets where the full metric set was computed, so it depends on neither the cutoff nor the use of a ranking-aware measure.

**The between-dataset difference is tested, not merely described.** For each collaborative baseline, Cohen's $d$ is bootstrapped independently within each dataset (10,000 resamples) and the distribution of the difference taken. All 8 of 8 contrasts are significant after BH correction, all 8 are sign flips, no confidence interval contains zero, and $\Delta d$ ranges from 0.58 to 0.83. See `results/domain_dependence_interaction.csv`.

**What sets a dataset's position is its cohort, not its domain.** Demonstrated within Last.fm-1K alone:

| Cohort | Users | Items heard by $\geq$2 users | Essence | CF-ItemKNN | $d$ | $q$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50–500 interactions (published) | 99 | 10.3% | 0.028075 | 0.001756 | **+0.281** | <0.0001 |
| 50–2,000 interactions | 374 | 28.4% | 0.002634 | 0.005475 | **−0.160** | 0.0139 |

Lifting the cap makes CF-ItemKNN the best of eight systems and drops Essence from 2nd to 5th. The same mechanism appears independently on Amazon Books, where heavy users degrade CF-ItemKNN by only 22% against 86–93% for content methods. **Confound disclosed:** lifting the cap raises user count *and* history length together, both acting through density; this dataset cannot separate them. See `COHORT_NOT_DOMAIN.md`.

> **Do not describe music as a weak-collaborative-signal domain.** Last.fm-1K's classification as sparse holds for the published 99-user cohort only.

## Key Findings

1. **Cohort density, not domain, and not a universal advantage.** See the tables above. On Amazon Books, Essence significantly beats CF-ItemKNN, the non-personalised baselines, Content and both neural baselines on Recall@10; it is not significantly different from Recency-Weighted or Avg-Last-10, and loses significantly only to Last-Item. On Last.fm-1K it beats the same set and is not significantly different from *any* recency-only baseline. On MovieLens-25M it loses to CF, Popularity and both neural baselines by the largest effect sizes in the study (Cohen's $d$ to $-0.55$). The earlier framing attributed this to the content domain; the controlled user-cap manipulation shows the mediator is cross-user co-occurrence density in the evaluated cohort, and the same music dataset sits on either side of the boundary depending on the cap.
2. **The clustering null, now unconditional.** A stratification test (`evaluation/clustering_value_test.py`) splits users by how well-separated their own clustering is (silhouette tertiles at the adopted $K$) and compares Essence against Recency-Weighted *within each stratum* — the users whose taste is most clearly multi-modal are where clustering should help most. At the adopted $K$, with tie-aware $p$-values and **no rows excluded, clustering is significant in none of 18 comparisons**. It also holds under a second sentence encoder (0 of 12 favour clustering), on heavy users with 330 interactions each at a re-validated $K{=}60$ ($q{=}0.31$, powered to detect 21.6%), on the expanded 374-user Last.fm cohort, under a global temporal cutoff, and across three $K$-means seeds and two user samples. Across the whole study — 136 hypothesis tests, BH-corrected study-wide — **no comparison anywhere favours clustering**, and the high-silhouette stratum shows clustering significantly *worse* ($q{=}0.033$). The earlier README reported "1 of 16 remains significant"; those sixteen were a hand-curated subset and the one significant row was an artifact of the defect in finding 3.
3. **A real statistical defect, found and fixed.** The two-sided bootstrap $p$-value was computed as $\min(1, 2\min(f, 1-f))$ with $f$ the fraction of resamples whose difference exceeds zero. That form counts every resample with a difference of *exactly* zero as evidence against the null. On sparse long-tail metrics, where most users score exactly 0, a one-sided-with-ties difference was therefore reported as maximally significant ($p{=}0$) — including the degenerate case where both arms were identically zero for every user. All call sites now count ties in both tails, the standard correction. **The effect is substantial and runs against this project's own claims:** the clustering-value test drops from 7 of 18 significant to 1 of 18, and to 0 of 18 at the adopted $K$. An earlier version of this README described excluding the fully degenerate cells from the significance family; that treated a symptom. The cells were degenerate because the formula was wrong, and the non-degenerate but tie-heavy cells it also inflated were left in.
4. **Cold-start vs. singleton long-tail is a scope boundary, not a contradiction.** Re-verified at the adopted $K$: content-only retrieval beats CF-ItemKNN in **5 of 6** coldest-decile comparisons, the exception being MovieLens-25M's second decile, where CF has begun to acquire signal. But this is not evidence for Essence's architecture specifically: running the identical test with Recency-Weighted, Last-Item or Avg-Last-10 in Essence's place, 15 of 18 comparisons are also significant (that check does not depend on Essence's $K$). The mechanism is mechanical — CF's co-occurrence score is exactly 0 for zero-interaction items by construction, so any content-embedding method clears that bar trivially. It is the same mechanism as finding 1, at the item level rather than the population level. See `results/scratch_family_C_coldstart_NEWK.csv`.
5. **The active-cluster-selection ablation, now on all three datasets.** Recency-based active-cluster selection (vs. a static full-history mean-embedding query) is retained as Essence's default mechanism: on Last.fm-1K, mean-select collapses below even the Content baseline on long-tail recall ($4.84\times$ LT/Content in recency-select's favour, up from $2.01\times$ at $K{=}3$); on Amazon Books the same pattern holds but less extremely. On MovieLens-25M the result is genuinely mixed — recency-select leads on Recall@10 but mean-select leads on LT-Recall@10 — reported as-is. **Scope:** these figures were computed at the earlier $K$ (10/10/15) and have **not** been re-run at the adopted $K$ (10/8/10); they are reported as the most recent verified numbers rather than assumed unchanged.
6. **The artist/author shortcut, re-verified at the adopted $K$ — and larger than published.** The published figures used a hardcoded $K{=}3$ and reproduce exactly at that $K$ (20.6% of hits sharing an artist on Last.fm-1K, an 87.9% collapse in Recall@10 when those candidates are excluded). At the adopted $K$ the dependence is **substantially larger: 32.9% of hits and a 95.3% collapse**. Restating it at the adopted $K$ sharpens rather than softens the limitation — a large part of what this architecture recovers is identity matching rather than taste modelling, and the shortcut grows with $K$. See `results/identity_shortcut_adoptedK.csv`.
7. **A caught implementation bug in MIND, not an architecture flaw.** An early version of the hand-implemented MIND baseline zero-initialized its routing logits, which — because MIND's bilinear routing matrix is shared across capsules — silently collapsed the model's effective interest count to 1 regardless of the nominal $K$. A synthetic conformance test caught this before any number in this repo was generated; see [MIND/ComiRec training and verification](#4-mindcomirec-training-and-verification).
8. **Multi-seed MIND/ComiRec robustness — superseded by baseline tuning.** At $K{=}3$, ComiRec (SA) beat Essence on 2 of 4 Last.fm-1K training seeds; at $K{=}10$, Essence's own Recall@10 rose enough ($0.0119 \to 0.0281$) that ComiRec no longer beat it on any seed (0/4). **That comparison has since been redone properly and the conclusion reversed.** Those seeds varied ComiRec's initialisation at a fixed learning rate; a validation-based tuning grid over learning rate and interest count closed the gap from $2.05\times$ to $1.15\times$, and under the conservative convention Essence is **no longer significantly ahead of ComiRec on Last.fm-1K** ($d{=}0.065$, $q{=}0.54$) — see finding 15. MIND never outranks Essence on Last.fm-1K or Amazon Books and always outranks it on MovieLens-25M, across every seed and every tuned configuration tested.
9. **A second, independent collaborative baseline (BPR-MF) replicates the same pattern exactly.** CF-ItemKNN is the only collaborative-filtering baseline behind the cohort-density finding above; to check this isn't an artifact of that one implementation, BPR-MF (Bayesian Personalized Ranking Matrix Factorization — a pairwise-ranking method sharing no code or architecture with ItemKNN) was implemented and conformance-tested on synthetic data before trusting any real-dataset number (the conformance test itself needed one correction — an overly strict pass/fail threshold — before all checks passed; see [MIND/ComiRec training and verification](#4-mindcomirec-training-and-verification) for the same discipline applied to the original neural baselines). Essence significantly beats BPR-MF where cohort co-occurrence is sparse (Last.fm-1K, $d{=}{+}0.296$; Amazon Books, $d{=}{+}0.261$) and significantly loses to it where collaborative signal is strong (MovieLens-25M, $d{=}{-}0.518$, BPR-MF ranking immediately behind CF-ItemKNN and ComiRec). BPR-MF's own LT-Recall@10 is $0.0000$ on all three datasets, including MovieLens-25M where it wins decisively on Recall@10. Full numbers: `results/scratch_bprmf_{lastfm,amazon,movielens}.csv` and `results/scratch_bprmf_bootstrap_{lastfm,amazon,movielens}.csv`.
10. **Recency-Weighted alone reproduces Essence's win/loss pattern on Recall@10 — but not on long-tail recall.** To check whether the pattern in Key Finding #1 is specific to $K$-means clustering or shared by the broader no-cross-user-pooling architecture family, Recency-Weighted (identical embeddings, no clustering) was tested against CF-ItemKNN, Popularity, MIND, and ComiRec the same way Essence was (paired bootstrap, BH-FDR, same family structure as the paper's existing methodology: Last.fm-1K/Amazon Books pooled, MovieLens-25M separate). On Recall@10 the pattern is identical: both significantly beat all four baselines where collaborative signal is weak (Last.fm-1K, Amazon Books, 4/4 each) and both lose significantly to all four where it is strong (MovieLens-25M, 4/4 each, $d$ down to $-0.539$ for Recency-Weighted vs. Essence's $-0.550$) — the pattern on Recall@10 belongs to the broader no-pooling architecture family, not to $K$-means clustering specifically. On LT-Recall@10 the pattern is weaker and flatter for Recency-Weighted (2/4 significant wins on every dataset, including MovieLens-25M, where Essence has none), and Essence is not significantly different from Recency-Weighted on LT-Recall@10 on any dataset ($q>0.35$ throughout) — reinforcing Key Finding #2: clustering shows no detectable long-tail advantage over simple recency-weighting on any dataset tested. Practical implication: a long-tail improvement is more likely to come from the embedding representation or retrieval mechanism than from the interest-selection/clustering step. See `paper/essence_paper_v2.tex`, Section 5.2, for the full numbers; this analysis was computed directly against `results/scratch_evaluation_results_K{10,15}_{lastfm,amazon,movielens}_merged.csv` using the same paired-bootstrap/BH-FDR methodology as `evaluation/paired_bootstrap.py` — **not yet saved as a standalone script**, a known reproducibility gap for this specific finding (unlike every other finding above, which has one).
11. **Power: the nulls are bounded, and some cells are not claimed.** A null is only informative if the test could have detected an effect, so `evaluation/power_analysis.py` computes the minimum detectable effect at 80% power for every null comparison, using the bootstrap standard error rather than a normal-theory approximation (tie-heavy paired differences are far from normal). Of 18 cells, **17 are informative and none reach their own MDE**. The comparisons split into two regimes: nulls on ranking metrics are adequately powered and carry content, while several long-tail cells cannot detect even a doubling of the comparator — those are **no longer asserted as nulls**, a correction to the earlier analysis, which did. See `results/null_power_analysis.csv`.
12. **Conclusions survive a second sentence encoder.** Replacing `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` (384-dim) with `all-mpnet-base-v2` (768-dim) leaves every conclusion intact: Essence improves slightly (Amazon Books 0.0246 → 0.0254, rank 3rd → 2nd), one of eighteen significance verdicts changes, and the selected $K$ is identical on all three datasets. As a correctness check on the replication itself, the five systems that do not consume item embeddings return **bit-identical** results while exactly the five that do consume them move.
13. **Sequential baselines, via a library rather than hand-implemented.** SASRec and GRU4Rec are evaluated through RecBole and validation-tuned, specifically to remove the "your baselines were hand-rolled" objection. Both land 21–175× below Essence on Last.fm-1K and Amazon Books. **This is not evidence Essence beats sequential models in general** — it reflects the structural sparsity of these cohorts (Amazon Books is 0.063% dense), which is the same property finding 1 identifies as the mediator. Disclosed: RecBole's split had to be an approximate time-ordered ratio split rather than consuming the pre-split files, and it agrees with ours on 92.8% of Amazon Books test items; the 7.2% are masked as history, very slightly penalising the sequential models.
14. **Temporal leakage: the disclosed limitation resolves in this project's favour.** Under a global timestamp cutoff rather than a per-user split, every system degrades, but not equally — CF-ItemKNN falls 86% and Popularity 62%, against 37% for Essence and 43% for Recency-Weighted. The leakage that the per-user protocol permits was inflating the collaborative baselines, exactly as the objection predicts, so the adopted protocol is the conservative choice for the comparison this project reports. The clustering null holds under this split too.
15. **Baselines are given the benefit of the doubt, deliberately, and it costs a result.** Every baseline is reported at the better of its published and its validation-tuned configuration. The most consequential consequence: Essence is no longer significantly ahead of ComiRec on Last.fm-1K ($d{=}0.065$, $q{=}0.54$) — tuning closed that gap from $2.05\times$ to $1.15\times$, and the earlier claim rested on a ComiRec denied a fair learning rate. That comparison is also underpowered (detectable effect 0.0159 against an observed 0.0038), so the two are **not shown to differ** at $n{=}99$ — never "equal". MovieLens baseline tuning was stopped at 2 of 24 configurations as a disclosed scope decision, since MIND and ComiRec already beat Essence there by 8–13× and tuning them further can only widen a gap already running against Essence.

## Known Limitations

These match what the paper itself states — see `paper/essence_paper_v2.tex`, Sections 5.6/6/7, for the full statements this summarizes:

- **Scale, and what the 99 users actually were.** Last.fm-1K's 99 users were the effect of an upper bound on user activity in the preprocessing filter, not the size of the available data; lifting it yields 374 users in the 50–2,000 band (and 975 uncapped), and doing so reverses the headline comparison — see [Headline Results](#headline-results-all-three-datasets). The main analysis still uses 99 users; its long-tail comparisons are directional (as few as 2 of 92 eligible users per method). Amazon Books and MovieLens-25M (2,000 users each) carry the statistical weight. A global-timestamp-cutoff split (tested as a robustness check, not adopted) strands 39% of Last.fm-1K's users (60/99 remain with data on both sides) and is uninformative at this scale — under it, Essence's Last.fm-1K standing genuinely reverses (significant losses to Recency-Weighted and Avg-Last-10, both currently reported as not significantly different, and LT-Recall@10 collapses to 0.0000). Amazon Books, run identically, held up close to its reported findings; MovieLens-25M strands 98.6% of users (28/2,000 remain) and is too underpowered to be informative either way. This is reported as a real, disclosed sensitivity of the per-user-split protocol at Last.fm-1K's scale specifically — the per-user-split results remain what this repository reports as findings.
- **Cross-user temporal leakage.** The chronological split is computed independently per user, with no shared global time cutoff. This preserves within-user chronology but does not enforce a global temporal cutoff; possible cross-user temporal leakage for population-based baselines (CF, Popularity) is a limitation of this evaluation protocol, not eliminated by it.
- **$K$-selection anchoring — resolved.** This was a real limitation and is no longer one. The stopping rule had been evaluated forward from an established $K{=}8$ checkpoint rather than from $K{=}2$, which is anchoring. It has been replaced by a criterion that is anchoring-free by construction (see [$K$-Selection Methodology](#k-selection-methodology)); two of the three adopted values changed as a result, and two further defects in the published configuration — an occupancy floor breach on Amazon Books and incomplete user coverage on MovieLens-25M — are now excluded by construction.
- **Rating threshold.** All Amazon and MovieLens ratings/reviews are treated as positive interactions regardless of rating value; this evaluates engagement/consumption prediction rather than preference prediction specifically. A sensitivity analysis using a positive-rating threshold (rating ≥ 4, verified against each dataset's actual scale — Amazon is 1–5 integer stars, MovieLens-25M is 0.5–5.0 half-stars) was performed as a robustness check; it did not reverse the cohort-density or clustering-null findings, though sample sizes drop 20–28% once users below the stricter floor are excluded.
- **MIND/ComiRec are hand-implemented, not pre-trained.** Both are trained from scratch per dataset rather than via production-tuned checkpoints; a well-tuned, larger-scale deployment could plausibly outperform the versions evaluated here, particularly on MovieLens-25M, where both already outrank Essence substantially.
- **The mechanism is now identified, but one confound remains.** An earlier version of this README listed "the domain-dependence mechanism is not fully explained" as an open limitation. It is now explained: the mediator is cross-user co-occurrence density, measured directly as the share of items consumed by two or more users, and manipulated within a single dataset rather than inferred across three. The remaining limitation is narrower — lifting Last.fm-1K's user cap raises user count *and* mean history length together, both acting through density, and this dataset contains too few users in the original 50–500 band to separate them. Density is claimed as the mediator; which of its causes dominates is not.
- **Additional robustness checks.** Multi-seed resampling (Amazon Books, MovieLens-25M) and validation-based ItemKNN hyperparameter tuning (neighbor count, shrinkage; test data never touched during the search) were each tested; neither reversed any reported finding's direction. CF-ItemKNN's Recall@10 on Amazon Books roughly doubles under validation-tuned shrinkage, but Essence still leads it by roughly $4\times$; on MovieLens-25M, tuning selected the existing untuned configuration as optimal (0% change). See [Reproducing the Results](#11-overnight-robustness-check-batch-rating-threshold-multi-seed-itemknn-tuning-global-cutoff-bpr-mf) for exact commands.
- **The identity shortcut is larger than the published figures suggested.** Re-verified at the adopted $K$: 32.9% of hits share an artist and excluding those candidates collapses Recall@10 by 95.3%, against 20.6% and 87.9% at the hardcoded $K{=}3$ originally reported. The shortcut grows with $K$, so this is a sharper limitation than before, not a resolved one — a substantial part of what the architecture recovers is identity matching rather than taste modelling.
- **The adaptive-weighting pilot is unvalidated** — see [above](#10-adaptive-weighting-pilot--exploratory-not-a-result).
